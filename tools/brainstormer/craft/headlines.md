# Headlines: the on-screen hook text

Used whenever an option or a brief needs the words on screen in the first 0–3 seconds. From the strategists'
`ad-copywriter` v2.1.0 (original in `archive/strategist-files-2026-11-03/`), rewritten for Bumpy: the original was
written for a local dating app, so its "nearby" examples are gone. Meta primary text and the headline under the ad
follow different evidence: see `playbook.md` → "Meta primary text and headlines".

**The only rule that matters:** if the text doesn't stop a stranger in 0.8 seconds, nothing else matters.

## 1. Map the persona before writing
Read the mind first (`mind.md`: beliefs, their words). Then answer:

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| What does this person want right now, tonight? | The desire the hook activates |
| What's the specific pain nobody has named yet? | Naming it = instant thumb-stop |
| What stops them believing it exists? | The objection the hook dismantles |

The hook activates the desire **or** names the pain. Never both: pick one. Write it **as the persona's own
thought**, not about them.
- ❌ "Dating locally is exhausting for women" (about them)
- ✅ "400 'hey's and not one real question" (their thought)

## 2. Find the viral phrase (it becomes the headline)
```
Dream outcome (1 word) + opposite of their biggest objection = viral phrase (2–5 words)
```

| Persona (mind) | Dream outcome | Biggest objection | Viral phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| NewLanguageLearner | Fluency | "No native wants to talk to me" | **Language Date** |
| LocalDatingFatigue (men) | Matches | "I'm just not attractive" | **Different Country, Different Standard** |
| GringoMaleLover-PT | A gringo boyfriend | "Foreign men don't want us" | **Mais gringo do que você imagina** |

A viral phrase is 2–5 words, self-explanatory, slightly absurd but accurate, repeatable in conversation without
explaining, and runs on Meta without flags. Reference phrases from other brands: Korean Facelift · BBL Leggings ·
Nature's Ozempic · Cortisol Lemonade.

## 3. Write the headline
The viral phrase is the headline; don't separate them. Read benchmarks as formulas, not words.
- **4–6 words** (flag anything longer).
- **Desire first:** never lead with the app name.
- **A distance word:** abroad, another country, worldwide, a country name or a flag. Never "nearby"
  (`core/brand.md`). "Not local" phrasing works too ("Maybe your person isn't local").
- **All caps or Title Case**, as TikTok text looks.
- **Clear who it's for and what they get:** a 7th grader gets it at once. Clear beats clever at 5 words.

Examples of the shape: `DATE SOMEONE FROM ANOTHER COUNTRY` · `LEARN KOREAN BY DATING A NATIVE 😏` ·
`MORE GRINGOS WANT LATINAS THAN YOU THINK` · `YOUR TYPE EXISTS, JUST NOT HERE`.
Too long, no phrase: `THERE ARE MEN IN TEN COUNTRIES WHO WANT EXACTLY THE SAME THING YOU WANT`.

## Meta-safe wordplay
When the real desire word is banned ("hook up"), use a phrase the audience gets at once and a moderator reads as
innocent, with the wink in one word: "a good time", "tonight 😏", "you know 😏". **Emoji rule:** one emoji, at the
end, only when it says what the words can't. 😏 implies more than friendship without saying it.

## Output
Per persona (never two personas in one headline): the viral phrase with its formula, the headline (winner + 1–2
alternatives, copy-paste ready), and one sentence on why it stops the scroll (the mechanism only).

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A full sentence | Cut to 4–6 words |
| Written about the persona | Write it as their thought |
| Phrase and headline separate | The phrase is the headline |
| Same formula for every persona | Each persona gets its own mechanism |
| Clever wordplay nobody gets | Clear beats clever |
| "Nearby" or no distance word at all | A country, a flag, "abroad" |
| A banned desire word | Meta-safe wordplay |

## Benchmarks and corrections
For each benchmark headline extract: word count; question, statement or command; the word doing the most work;
what's implied but not said. Match the formula, not the words.
Corrections are the most valuable input: understand *why* before turning one into a rule, apply it to the rest of
the session, and add it to `playbook.md` → "Rules learned" if it will come up again.
