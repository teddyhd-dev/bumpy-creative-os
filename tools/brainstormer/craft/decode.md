# Decode a signal or an ad

Used by the brainstormer (SKILL.md step 2 and whenever the strategist shares a signal). Merged on 3 Nov 2026 from
the strategists' `ad-signal-decoder` v2.0.0 (visual read, organic signals, persona map) and the older
`ad-script-decoder` v1 (flow, tempo and rhythm; repurposing). Originals in
`archive/strategist-files-2026-11-03/`.

A **signal** is any content that is working: the platform surfaces it, people share it, it gets comments. A paid
ad script, a TikTok's on-screen text, a screenshot, an organic post or comment, a set of stills from one video, a
spreading meme format. Organic viral content is as valuable as paid ads: if the platform pushes it, the emotional
mechanic is proven. Hook first, always: everything else depends on whether the first 1.5 seconds work.

## 1. Read the visual first (when there is an image or video)
| Extract | Question |
|---|---|
| Setting | Where is this? What does the place say about the person's state? |
| Body language / expression | What emotion is performed or implied? |
| Camera angle / shot type | Selfie = confessional trust. Wide = aspiration. POV = intimacy. |
| Production level | Raw and handheld = organic, trusted. Polished = brand, skeptical. |
| What moves or is implied | Walking = a decision in motion. On a plane = already committed. Staring = longing. |

The visual sets the emotion; the text lands inside it. If they contradict, the content fails; if they amplify each
other, it converts.

## 2. Decode the hook (first 1.5 seconds, visual + text)
- **Surface formula.** Which template? e.g. `When [relatable situation] so you [extreme reaction]` · `[Situation]
  so bad I'm on my way to [unexpected place]` · `You [casual action] and now you're [unexpected consequence]` ·
  `[Identity] ditch [X], do [Y] instead` · `The [noun] these days…` · `If you want [X], this is for you` ·
  `Most [identity] don't know about [X]`.
- **Psychological mechanism** (name the mechanism in the brain, not the effect): identity activation ("that's
  me" makes a micro-pause, and the pause is watch time) · desire surfacing · shared complaint · pattern interrupt ·
  curiosity gap · absurd escalation (humor = share) · loss aversion (twice as strong as a gain frame).
- **Platform mechanic:** loopable · text-first (works with sound off) · shareable ("I need to send this") ·
  reaction-bait · visual metaphor (the image alone stops the scroll).
- **Conversion gap** to "try Bumpy": tiny (it already expresses the desire Bumpy fulfils) · medium (names the
  problem, points nowhere) · large (a feeling Bumpy can own, needs a bridge scene). Bigger gap = more scenes.

## 3. Body decode (scripts and multi-scene content only)
For each beat after the hook: which objection it removes (name it exactly), which lever it pulls (social proof,
outcome proof, identity upgrade, ease, loss aversion), and why this order (what the viewer must believe before the
next beat lands; would it fail if two beats swapped? where does it earn the right to ask?).

## 4. Flow, tempo and rhythm (spoken scripts)
Scripts are heard, not read.
- **Sentence length pattern:** short = speed and urgency; long = trust. Map it (short → short → long → short?).
- **Conversational markers:** "listen", "right?", "honestly": trust signals that make scripted speech feel
  unscripted. Which appear, and why they work.
- **Pause architecture:** where punctuation, rhythm breaks or questions force a micro-pause.
- **Emotional temperature:** cold → warm, or hot all the way? Strong scripts often go neutral hook → recognition →
  logical proof → emotional close.

## 5. Map the persona and the mind
Who is expressing this (gender, age, relation to dating apps), what they want right now, what objection stops them
from trying international dating. Name the persona from `core/naming-adset-v8.md` (never an old spelling, never a
new name) and the mind (persona × language, `brain/minds/`). If it opens a new persona, say so in one sentence and
flag it to the creative lead; don't invent a name.

## 6. Pattern report (3+ signals)
Dominant formula · the emotional trigger present in all of them (non-negotiable in any adaptation) · the visual
mechanic that keeps appearing · missing triggers (highest-upside tests) · conversion-gap spread · the weakest line
in each (where an adaptation should improve first).

## 7. Repurpose (when asked, or when the decode feeds options)
Hand over to `craft/options.md`: the signal is the brief. For a spoken script, write each new version as
`HOOK → V1 (objection removed) → V2 (lever) → CTA (friction)`, with 2–3 proven-formula versions and 1–2 new-angle
versions clearly labelled as bets.

## Where the decode goes
- **Our own ad** (a current or past #1): the Decode section of `brain/creatives/<code>-<name>.md` (first second,
  hook, message, format, why it stops the thumb, full breakdown, snapshot, decoded by) and a `decode` entry in the
  mind's `history.md` saying why it works, or stopped working, *for this mind*.
- **Someone else's signal:** the mind's "Inspiration bank" (link · numbers · why · who, date) and an `inspiration`
  entry in `history.md`.

## Output order
1. Visual read (skip if text only) · 2. Hook decode · 3. Conversion gap · 4. Persona and mind · 5. Body decode
(multi-scene only) · 6. Flow and tempo (spoken scripts only) · 7. Pattern report (3+ signals only).

## Standards
- Specific, never generic. "This creates curiosity" is not analysis. "The viewer can't tell if this is an ad or a
  real story, so they watch past second 3 to find out" is.
- Say what's weak: top spenders often have a weak second beat or a lazy CTA.
- Label what's proven (from the input) vs what's a bet.
