# Refresh playbook

Every option the brainstormer proposes is one of these moves. The move is written into the experiment
record (`move:`), so over time we learn which move works for which mind.

| Move (`move:` value) | What changes | What stays | When it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Same message, new visual** (`same-message-new-visual`) | The first-second visual: actor, setting, prop, framing | Concept, message, headline | The #1 is fading but its message still lands. Meta rewards ads that look different: a new visual reaches people the old one never stopped. E.g. *wanted*: new actor, same sign. |
| **Same message, new format** (`same-message-new-format`) | The format or style: UGC, carousel, product demo, founder, static, talking head, text-on-video | The winning message theme | The message is proven but the format is worn. Pick formats that won in other minds (see each creative's "Where it ran") or in strong ad-library / social references. |
| **Same visual, new angle** (`same-visual-new-angle`) | The angle or message theme | The hook visual or concept style | The visual still stops thumbs but the promise has tired. Ground the new angle in the mind's beliefs and its own words (forums, comments: quote them). |
| **New angle** (`new-angle`) | Everything | Only the mind | No winner to build on, or the mind's beliefs suggest an untested desire. Needs inspiration from the strategist (ad library, social, brain dump). |
| **Proven elsewhere** (`proven-elsewhere`, J1) | The audience | The concept, localised | A concept is #1 in another mind or language and never ran here (the ticket's `j1` list). Localise the words; keep the hook. |
| **Past winner, new form** (`past-winner-new-form`, J2) | The execution of a past #1 of this adset | Its core idea | A past #1 here faded long ago; its idea may be fresh again for new people. |

## Rules from our data (back-tests on our exports since Dec 2025)
- **One concept per refresh, 3+ variants per adset.** A new concept with 3+ variants in the adset became
  #1 40% of the time vs 15% with 1–2. Variants keep the code: a new hook sub-type (same kind of opening, another
  actor or prop), new text, a re-cut, a V2 (scenes reordered after the same opening), another language.
- **Milk every generation.** Plan the extra outputs in the brief: swap the on-screen text, re-cut, V2 re-orders,
  make the IMG from the strongest frame. A version that opens on a different scene (e.g. starts at the reveal) is a
  **new concept code** (`core/naming-ad-v5.md`): no extra generation, but it is judged as its own concept.
- **Spread one concept across the mind's adsets.** A concept lands in a median 3 adsets; in 5+ adsets it
  became #1 somewhere 71% of the time vs 25% in one. Each adset keeps its own result.
- **Localise per language; one brief covers both OS.** Results differed by language for 42% of concepts
  tried in 2+ languages; iOS and Android agreed 80% of the time.
- **Judge at week 1, decide at week 4.** 20%+ of the adset's spend in week 1 usually means a future #1.
  A third of winners started under 5%: keep quiet ads on.
- **Brief the successor early.** A #1 leads a median 3 weeks and ROAS slides from week 3; a refresh takes
  about 2 weeks. Due tickets exist for this.
- **Strikes count only when the adset was under the bar.** Missing against a healthy #1 is a foul.

## Copy formulas seen in past winners
Examples to learn from, not templates to fill. Each worked for a specific mind at a specific time; the mind's
"Messages that land" and facts block say where. From the team's `bumpy-knowledge.md` v1.1 (Nov 2026).

1. **More than you think** (kills the scarcity doubt): "There are more [foreign men] wanting to date [nationality]
   women than you think." The most common objection is "nobody like me wants someone like me".
2. **Flag + nationality:** "Highly recommend falling in love with a 🇮🇹 man on Bumpy." Flags are visual shorthand.
3. **POV discovery:** "POV: you didn't realize guys abroad are lining up for Filipinas on Bumpy." The viewer's own
   realisation, written as them.
4. **Two-slide turn (fun… until):** "Chatting with a foreigner is fun… until" → "He booked a flight to come see you ❤️".
   The emotion lives in the gap between the slides.
5. **Social-proof timeline:** "February 2025 — Matched with a British Muslim guy on Bumpy" → "February 2026 — Are you
   free this Valentine's?" → "He came to see me". Works for hesitant personas.
6. **Outcome + flag:** "I'm moving to my new bf's country 🇸🇪 3 months after using Bumpy." Flag = specific;
   timeline = believable.
7. **Paradox authority:** "How to fix zero matches. From a guy with a 0% match rate on Tinder." Credibility comes from
   the failure. Superseded for LocalDatingFatigue men by 8; kept for the female side.
8. **CTE broken grammar** (myth-break): telegraphic grammar from the "[Topic] but we both have CTE 🧠" series. Opener
   naming the pain as a diagnosis → the app's promise killed in one word (`"…" Lie.`) → the mechanism ("celebrity
   like" profiles pushed, everyone else buried) → "Your fault? Not really. Your fault is limiting your options to
   small pool." → small pool vs big pool → `0.00001%` vs `99.999%` → "Stop believe dating app promise. Download
   Bumpy. Match today." Ran as concept Q3xLm8 (cte), Batch2026Week34; scripts in `brain/creatives/q3xlm8-cte.md`.

Writing rules that came with them: flags beat words on screen; name the nationality, country or language (on-screen
hooks; Meta headlines differ, below); local-language copy beats English for non-English personas; "more than you
think" answers any scarcity doubt; the friends, language or faith angle is always a door to dating.

## Meta primary text and headlines (internal data, Oct 2026)
From `bumpy-learnings.md`: one week, about $74k of headline spend, account ROAS 0.18, IPM 0.59, read in the order
Spend → ROAS → IPM, plus a Meta Ad Library review (impression rank as a proxy for what Meta spends on).
⚠ These are the headlines' own ROAS rows. The spec (§6.2) treats a creative's own ROAS as weak evidence; read them as
directions, not proof. (Open question for the creative lead, `core/decisions.md`.)
- What Meta spends on ≠ what pays: the top two spenders were among the worst payers. Aim for lines Meta spends on
  *and* that clear ROAS. Best general lines at scale: "Done With Local Dating? Try the World." (IPM 1.15), "Love
  Beyond Borders." (most installs).
- Abstract borders and culture lines beat naming a nationality in headlines (0.25 ROAS / 0.83 IPM vs 0.18 / 0.49).
- "Not local" inversion is the strongest signal: JP 運命の人は近くにいるとは限らない (1.07 / 2.57); PT "Talvez Ele Não
  Seja Local". "Not local" works; "foreigner" flopped (0.04).
- No numbers in headlines ("200+ countries", "20M singles" were the weakest theme). No CTA in the headline (it sits
  next to the Install button; "Download Bumpy" did worse). Three-word "X. Y. Z." lists fail (0.12).
- Emoji: slight lift. Questions: higher IPM at the same ROAS. The brand name adds nothing.
- Natural sentence length beats a clipped slogan. End on her, not on an instruction ("She might be in Asia." >
  "Try dating in Asia"). A "worth it" sacrifice must be specific and persona-true ("worth learning a whole new
  alphabet for" > "worth the long flight").
- Mix method: keep the exact phrase from a current winner when it already fits a formula ("maybe she never was");
  rebuild the rest.
- Primary text: only about 125 characters show before "…more"; the first line must work alone.

## Rules learned
Add a line when a strategist corrects the brainstormer on something that will come up again
(date · who · rule). Lines up to 2026-11-03 marked "learnings" come from the strategists' `bumpy-learnings.md`
(Nov 2026) and `bumpy-knowledge.md`.

**How to take a correction:** don't turn every correction into a hard rule. Understand *why* it was given; most come
back to understanding the signal. Stay close to it, and don't overbuild: it's a simple job.

- 2026-11 · learnings · If an option could exist without having seen the signal, it's wrong.
- 2026-11 · learnings · Logic-check every line ("Now I'm flying to 🇸🇪 to meet someone I met on there" doesn't follow).
- 2026-11 · learnings · Two scenes with the same text are one scene. Short and striking beats long and high-context,
  even with many scenes.
- 2026-11 · learnings · UGC talking-head scripts need natural imperfection: thoughts run into each other, no full stop
  that kills momentum mid-thought. Rejected drafts were "long and choppy, not human".
- 2026-11 · learnings · One small specific moment beats a list (one night, one topic, one reaction).
- 2026-11 · learnings · One CTA, not two stacked at the end.
- 2026-11 · learnings · The hook must touch the real wound. A generic social dynamic ("popularity decides what's
  cool") was rejected as not triggering: go one layer more personal.
- 2026-11 · learnings · Gender-neutral callouts in ES/PT use the masculine plural: `Mis cristianos solteros…` /
  `Meus cristãos solteiros…`.
- 2026-11 · learnings · "Nearby" used contrastively is still flagged; the strategist decides (`core/brand.md`).
- 2026-11 · learnings · Mega-Brief for sets across languages; no rationale in briefs; re-sequenced openings are
  separate concepts; statics from video stills cost 0 generation; handwritten text in reused clips can't be
  translated by overlay; check map pins (one frame shipped centred on India). Now in `craft/brief-writer.md`.
- 2026-08 · knowledge · LocalDatingFatigue men: use the CTE broken-grammar format (formula 8), not paradox authority.
  No montage, no explainer sentences, no borrowed reaction lines: every line is a fact, a promise-kill or a number.

- 2026-11-02 · TED · A concept moved into another persona's adset (proven elsewhere) gets its own copy named for
  that persona, same concept code, so each audience is credited correctly.
- 2026-11-02 · TED · Pick new concept codes from `core/concepts.csv` (unique ignoring letter case); write the
  concept name exactly as it is there, and put scene detail in the hook field.
