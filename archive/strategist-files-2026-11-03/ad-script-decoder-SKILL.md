# Ad Signal Decoder

A framework for reverse-engineering what makes content stop scrolls and convert —
applied to any format: script, headline, screenshot, image, video still, or a set of them.

Hook first, always. Everything else is downstream of whether the first 1.5 seconds work.

---

## What a "Signal" Is

A signal is any piece of content from the internet that is working — meaning the platform
is surfacing it, people are sharing it, or it's generating comments and reactions.

Signals can be:
- A paid ad script or voiceover
- A headline or on-screen text from a TikTok
- A screenshot or image with text overlay
- An organic post, caption, or comment that went viral
- A set of images/screenshots from the same video or trend
- A meme format that's spreading

**Organic viral content is as valuable as paid ads.** If the platform is pushing it,
the emotional mechanic is proven. The job is to decode what mechanic is working and
why — then use it.

---

## Step 1: Read the Visual First

When a signal includes an image or screenshot, decode the visual before the text.

| What to extract | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| **Setting** | Where is this? What does the location signal about the person's state? |
| **Body language / expression** | What emotion is being performed or implied? |
| **Camera angle / shot type** | Selfie = confessional trust. Wide shot = aspiration. POV = intimacy. |
| **Production level** | Raw and handheld = organic/trusted. Polished = brand/skeptical. |
| **What's moving or implied** | Walking = decision in motion. Sitting on plane = already committed. Staring = longing. |

The visual creates the emotional context. The text lands inside that context.
If the visual and text contradict each other, the content fails. If they amplify each other, it converts.

---

## Step 2: Decode the Hook

The hook is the first 1.5 seconds — visual + text together.

### 2A. Surface Formula
What structural template is this using?
- `"When [relatable situation] so you [extreme reaction]"` → frustration + absurd escalation
- `"[Situation] so bad I'm on my way to [unexpected place/person]"` → desperation + humor
- `"You [casual action] and now you're [unexpected consequence]"` → low-effort cause, high-reward effect
- `"[Identity] ditch [X], do [Y] instead"` → direct address + command redirect
- `"The [noun] these days..."` → shared complaint, visual metaphor
- `"If you want [X] — this is for you"` → identity filter
- `"Most [identity] don't know about [X]"` → knowledge gap + insider signal

### 2B. Psychological Mechanism
What is happening in the viewer's brain in the first 1.5 seconds?

- **Identity activation** — "that's me" creates a micro-pause. The brain hesitates to scroll while confirming the match. That hesitation is watch time.
- **Desire surfacing** — naming an unarticulated desire creates an "oh wait" moment. Recognition fires before the conscious mind can dismiss.
- **Shared complaint** — "I feel that too" triggers the urge to share. Comment bait and save bait both live here.
- **Pattern interrupt** — something visually or textually unexpected forces the brain to stop and process before it can choose to scroll.
- **Curiosity gap** — the hook implies more is coming and the brain cannot scroll until it knows.
- **Absurd escalation** — a relatable problem + an extreme reaction creates humor. Humor = share.
- **Loss aversion** — implying the viewer is already missing something is 2x more motivating than a gain frame.

### 2C. Platform Mechanic
Why is the algorithm surfacing this?
- **Loopable** — end of video loops back to beginning naturally, inflating watch time
- **Text-first** — readable with sound off, high completion rate
- **Shareable** — "this is me" or "I need to send this to someone" = distribution signal
- **Reaction-bait** — content that makes people comment "same" or tag friends
- **Visual metaphor** — image alone stops the scroll before text registers

### 2D. Conversion Gap
How far is the emotion this content creates from "try Bumpy"?
- **Tiny gap** — content is already expressing the desire Bumpy fulfills (e.g. "I'd rather date in another country")
- **Medium gap** — content expresses the problem Bumpy solves but doesn't point anywhere (e.g. "dating pool is bad")
- **Large gap** — content expresses a feeling Bumpy can own but requires a bridge scene to connect

Smaller gap = fewer scenes needed. Larger gap = need more narrative to make the redirect feel logical.

## Step 3: Persona Mapping

After decoding the signal, identify which Bumpy persona this content is speaking to.

Ask:
- Who is expressing this frustration or desire? (gender, age range, relationship to dating apps)
- What do they want right now, specifically?
- What objection are they carrying that's stopping them from trying international dating?
- Which existing Bumpy persona matches this? Or is this a new persona?

**Known Bumpy personas** (from bumpy-knowledge.md; canonical names in bumpy-brief-writer-SKILL.md):

Korean Male Lover · Christian Worldwide · Language Learner · Dating App Refugee (local dating app users who are burned out)

If the signal maps to a known persona, name it.
If the signal opens a new persona angle, name it and describe it in one sentence.

---

## Step 4: Body Decode (for scripts and multi-scene content only)

When the signal has more than a hook — a full script, multiple scenes, or a sequence of images — decode the rest as a value chain. Each beat earns the next.

For each beat after the hook:
- **What objection does this remove?** Name it exactly.
- **What psychological lever does this pull?** (social proof, outcome proof, identity upgrade, ease, loss aversion)
- **Why this order?** What does the viewer need to believe before the next beat lands?

---

## Step 5: Multi-Signal Pattern Report (when 3+ signals provided)

When given multiple signals at once:
- **Dominant formula** — which hook structure appears most? What does that tell us about the audience?
- **Consistent emotional trigger** — what feeling appears across all signals? This is non-negotiable in any adaptation.
- **Consistent visual mechanic** — what shot type or setting keeps appearing? This is what the platform is rewarding right now.
- **Missing angles** — what proven triggers are absent from the set? Highest-upside test opportunities.
- **Conversion gap distribution** — are most signals tiny-gap or medium-gap? This tells us how much narrative work our ads need to do.

---

## Output Format

Always in this order:

1. **VISUAL READ** — what the image/screenshot communicates before text
2. **HOOK DECODE** — formula, psychological mechanism, platform mechanic
3. **CONVERSION GAP** — tiny / medium / large + one sentence on why
4. **PERSONA MAP** — which Bumpy persona this signals to, and why
5. **BODY DECODE** — only if multi-scene or full script
6. **PATTERN REPORT** — only if 3+ signals

If the user provides only a headline or one-liner with no visual, skip Step 1 and go deep on Step 2.

---

## Standards

- Be specific. "This creates curiosity" is not analysis. "This creates a curiosity gap because the viewer can't determine if this is an ad or a real story, forcing them to watch past second 3 to resolve the ambiguity" is analysis.
- Name the mechanism in the brain, not just the effect.
- Organic viral content is not less valuable than paid ads. The platform is the proof.
- When multiple images come from the same video, read them as a sequence — the relationship between scenes is as important as each scene individually.
