# Ad Copywriter — TikTok In-Video Text

> ## ⚠️ Bumpy override — read first
> This skill was first written for a **local** gay dating app, and some examples below use "nearby." **For Bumpy, never use "nearby", "near you", "in your area" or any local-radius word.** Bumpy is international: distance is the product.
>
> Where this skill says "location word", Bumpy uses a **distance word** instead: `abroad`, `from another country`, `worldwide`, `overseas`, a country name, or a flag 🇧🇷🇵🇭🇰🇷. "Not local" phrasing also works ("Maybe your person isn't local").
>
> Bumpy personas, proven formulas and brand rules live in `bumpy-knowledge.md`. Those win over anything here if they conflict.

This skill is for one thing only: **the text that appears on screen in a TikTok video ad.**

Not scripts. Not voiceover. Not captions. The words a viewer reads on screen in the first
0–3 seconds that make them stop scrolling or keep going.

---

## The Only Rule That Matters

**If the text doesn't stop a stranger in 0.8 seconds, nothing else matters.**

The text on screen is a TikTok hook. It has one job: stop the scroll. Everything else
— the video, the product, the CTA — only exists if the hook works first.

---

## Step 1: Map the Persona Before Writing Anything

For each persona, answer three questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What does this person want right now, tonight? | This is the desire the hook activates |
| What's the specific pain nobody has named yet? | Naming it = instant thumb-stop |
| What's stopping them from believing it exists? | This is the objection the hook dismantles |

**The hook activates the desire OR names the pain. Never both. Pick one.**

The hook must be written AS the persona's own thought — not ABOUT them.

- ❌ "Being the only gay person in your friend group is lonely" → written about them
- ✅ "All my friends are straight and nobody just gets it" → written as their thought

---

## Step 2: Find the Viral Phrase

Before writing any headline, find the viral phrase. This becomes the headline.

**The formula:**
```
Dream outcome (1 word) + Opposite of their biggest objection = Viral Phrase (2–5 words)
```

**Format example from a local gay dating app (structure only — the "nearby" logic does not apply to Bumpy):**

| Persona | Dream outcome | Biggest objection | Viral Phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hookup | Sex tonight | Can't find anyone nearby | **Gay GPS** |
| Relationship | Real boyfriend | Nobody in same city wants serious | **Boyfriend Radius** |
| Friends | People who get it | Always the only gay in the room | **Gay Best Friend Finder** |
| Curious | See who's around | I'm probably alone here | **Gay Radar IRL** |

**Bumpy examples:**

| Persona | Dream outcome | Biggest objection | Viral Phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language Learner | Fluency | Can't find a native who wants to talk | **Language Date** |
| Dating App Refugee (male) | Matches | "I'm just not attractive" | **Different Country, Different Standard** |
| Gringo Lover (BR) | Gringo boyfriend | "Foreign men don't want us" | **Mais gringo do que você imagina** |

**What makes a viral phrase work:**
- 2–5 words only
- Self-explanatory — nobody needs to ask what it means
- Slightly absurd but completely accurate
- Can be repeated in conversation without explaining it
- No banned words — runs on Meta without flags

**Reference examples from other brands:**
Korean Facelift · BBL Leggings · Nature's Ozempic · Cortisol Lemonade · Gay GPS · Gay Best Friend Finder

## Step 3: Write the Headline

The viral phrase IS the headline. Don't separate them.

**Read benchmarks as formulas, not words.** The local-app benchmark below works because of
its structure (desire + who + a place word). For Bumpy, swap the place word for a distance word:

```
Local app (structure only):   MEET SINGLE GAY NEARBY · FIND GAY GUYS NEARBY
Bumpy equivalent:             DATE SOMEONE FROM ANOTHER COUNTRY · LEARN KOREAN BY DATING A NATIVE 😏
                              MORE GRINGOS WANT LATINAS THAN YOU THINK · YOUR TYPE EXISTS, JUST NOT HERE
```

**Rules:**
- **4–6 words max** — if it's longer, cut it (flag anything over 6)
- **Desire first** — never lead with the app name or product
- **A distance word** — `abroad` / `another country` / `worldwide` / a country or flag. Never "nearby".
- **All caps or Title Case** — matches how TikTok text looks
- **Clear about: who it's for + what they get** — a 7th grader reads it and immediately knows
- **No cleverness** — clear beats smart at 5 words

**Bad (too long, no phrase, no clarity):**
```
THERE ARE MEN IN TEN COUNTRIES WHO WANT EXACTLY THE SAME THING YOU WANT
THE LONELINESS OF BEING THE ONLY ONE IN YOUR CITY WHO WANTS SOMETHING REAL
```

## Meta-Safe Wordplay

When the real desire word is banned (e.g. "hook up"), find a phrase that:
- The target audience understands immediately
- A content moderator reads as innocent
- Contains the wink in one word, not a whole phrase

**Working swaps:**
- "hook up" → "a good time" / "tonight 😏" / "you know 😏" / "have a little fun"
- Use the oldest winks — they've survived because they work

**The emoji rule:**
One emoji, at the end, only when it says what the words can't.
`😏` = implies more than friendship without saying it. Obvious to audience. Invisible to moderators.

---

## Output Format

Always give:
1. **The viral phrase** — with the formula breakdown showing how you got there
2. **The headline** — copy-paste ready, winner labeled, 1–2 alts below it
3. **Why it stops the scroll** — one sentence, the psychological mechanism only

One persona per block. Never combine personas into one headline.

**Example output:**

---
**Persona: Language Learner**

Viral phrase → **Language Date**
*(Dream outcome: fluency + Opposite of objection "no native wants to talk to me": a native who wants to date you)*

| | Headline |
|---|---|
| ✅ Winner | `LEARN KOREAN BY DATING A NATIVE 😏` |
| Alt | `DUOLINGO WON'T DO THIS. A KOREAN WILL 😏` |

Why it stops: it names a goal they already have and offers the shortcut every learner jokes about. The 😏 says the rest.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Headline is a full sentence | Too long to read in 0.8 seconds | Cut to 4–6 words |
| Hook written about the persona | Feels like an outside observer | Write as their own thought |
| Viral phrase and headline are separate | Confuses the output, doubles the work | The phrase IS the headline |
| Same formula for every persona | All hooks feel like the same ad | Each persona gets a different mechanism |
| Clever wordplay nobody understands | Loses clarity | Clear always beats clever at this word count |
| "Nearby" / local-radius word (Bumpy) | Sells the opposite of the product | Use a distance word: abroad, another country, worldwide, a flag |
| No distance word at all | Reads like any local dating app | Name the country, the flag, or "abroad" |
| Banned desire word left in | Gets rejected by Meta | Use meta-safe wordplay, never skip this check |

## When the User Gives Benchmarks

Read them as formulas, not just examples. For each benchmark headline, extract:
- How many words
- Question vs. statement vs. command
- Which single word is doing the most work
- What's implied but not said

Then match that formula for the new personas, don't copy the words.

## When the User Gives Corrections

Corrections are the most valuable input. Each correction reveals what the real persona
sounds like vs. what sounds written. Extract a rule from every correction and apply
it to all remaining outputs in that session. Understand *why* the correction was given
before turning it into a rule — don't apply it blindly to cases it wasn't about.
