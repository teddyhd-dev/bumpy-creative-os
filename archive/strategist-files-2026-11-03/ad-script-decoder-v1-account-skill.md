# Ad Script Decoder

A framework for reverse-engineering what makes paid social ads convert — with a strict
hierarchy: hook first, always. Everything else is downstream of whether the door opens.

This skill applies to any paid ad format: UGC video scripts, static ad copy, hook
headlines, carousel text, one-liners. The input can be one line or twenty. The output
is always a deep decode + actionable repurposing options.

---

## The Mental Model: Door-to-Door Salesman

Think of every ad as a door-to-door salesman pitch:

- **The hook** = the first words when the door opens. One chance. If the door closes, nothing else matters.
- **Value 1** = the first reason they don't close the door. Removes the biggest objection or amplifies the desire.
- **Value 2** = the second reason. Stacks the case. Should feel like it would be unfair NOT to try this.
- **CTA** = the close. One action. No friction.

The structure is always: `[Hook] → [V1] → [V2] → [CTA]`

Most viewers see 2–4 seconds of an ad. The hook IS the ad for most people. Analyze it accordingly.

## Step 1: Hook Isolation

**Always start here. Treat the hook as a completely separate unit.**

Identify the exact hook — it is always the first 3–10 words or the first sentence. Do not blend it with the body.

For each hook, decode:

### 1A. The Surface Formula
What structural template is this hook using? Common formulas:
- `"if you want [X] — this is for you"` → identity filter
- `"[App] is like [known thing] but for [specific identity]"` → comparison anchor
- `"do you want to [desire] [proximity]?"` → desire + proximity
- `"listen, I'm [live result] right now"` → pattern interrupt + live proof
- `"[number] [people/thing] are [doing X] near you"` → social proof + FOMO
- `"most [identity] don't know about [X]"` → knowledge gap + insider signal

### 1B. The Psychological Mechanism (go deep here)
What is actually happening in the viewer's brain in the first 2 seconds? Be specific:

- **Identity activation**: Does the hook make the viewer think "that's me" or "that's not me"? Identity hooks create a micro-pause — the viewer hesitates to scroll while their brain confirms or denies the match. That hesitation is watch time. That watch time is an algorithm signal.
- **Desire surfacing**: Does the hook name something the viewer already wants but hasn't articulated? Naming an unarticulated desire creates an "oh wait" moment — the brain registers recognition before the conscious mind can dismiss it.
- **Pattern interrupt**: Does the hook break the visual/audio pattern of everything else in the feed? "Listen," or "I'm on a date right now" forces an involuntary attention shift. The brain cannot choose to ignore a pattern interrupt — it has to process it first.
- **Proximity trigger**: Words like "nearby," "in your area," "right now," "near you" activate the brain's local-opportunity detection. These words are processed the same way as a local search query — high intent, low friction to act.
- **Loss aversion**: Is there something the viewer is implicitly missing by NOT clicking? Loss aversion is 2x more motivating than equivalent gain. Hooks that imply "you're already missing this" outperform "you could have this."
- **Curiosity gap**: Does the hook create an information gap that the brain needs to close? The viewer cannot scroll away until they know what comes next.

### 1C. Why the Algorithm Rewards This Hook
Meta and TikTok/Reels platforms rank ads on engagement signals — not intent. Decode what the hook produces algorithmically:
- **Thumb-stop rate**: What specifically causes the thumb to stop? (visual cue called out in script, audio pattern, opening word)
- **Watch time signal**: Does the hook create involuntary watch time? (curiosity gap, "is this real?", pattern interrupt all produce this)
- **Comment/share bait**: Does the hook make people want to react publicly? ("is this real?", "I felt seen", "this is me" all drive comments)
- **CTR signal**: Does the hook pre-qualify clickers? A hook that filters identity means only high-intent people click — low CTR but high conversion rate, which trains the algorithm toward better audiences.

### 1D. Target Audience Fit
Why does this specific hook land for THIS specific audience (not audiences in general)?
- What belief, fear, desire, or frustration does this audience carry that the hook activates?
- What makes this audience uniquely susceptible to this psychological mechanism?
- What would make this hook fail for a different audience?

## Step 2: Body Decode (only after hook is fully decoded)

Now analyze the rest of the script as a value chain. Each sentence earns the next one.

For each value beat after the hook:

### 2A. What objection does this remove?
Every value beat exists to kill a reason NOT to act. Name the exact objection it removes:
- "apps don't work" → overcome with outcome proof
- "nobody near me uses it" → overcome with proximity social proof
- "it's just another hookup app" → overcome with identity upgrade framing
- "I'll just check it out later" → overcome with urgency or loss framing

### 2B. What psychological lever does this pull?
- Social proof (others are doing this)
- Authority (this is legitimate/popular/validated)
- Outcome proof (real result, real person, real time)
- Identity upgrade (using this makes me the kind of person I want to be)
- Ease/speed removal of effort objection ("fast," "easy," "3 days")

### 2C. Information sequence — why this order?
The order of values is not arbitrary. Decode why this specific sequence works:
- What does the viewer need to believe before the next sentence lands?
- Would the script fail if V1 and V2 were swapped? Why?
- Where does the script earn permission to make the ask (CTA)?

---

## Step 3: Flow, Tempo & Rhythm Analysis

This is what most analyses miss. Scripts are heard, not read. Decode:

### 3A. Sentence length pattern
Short sentences create speed and urgency. Long sentences slow down and build trust. 
Map the pattern: is it short → short → long → short (builds then closes)? Or consistent throughout?

### 3B. Conversational markers
Words like "listen," "right?", "well," "honestly," "I'm serious" — these are trust signals that make scripted speech feel unscripted. Note which ones appear and why they work.

### 3C. Pause architecture
Where does the script force a mental pause? Punctuation, rhythm breaks, and rhetorical questions all create micro-pauses where the viewer's brain processes and re-commits to watching.

### 3D. Emotional temperature curve
Does the script start cold (informational) and warm up (emotional)? Or start hot and sustain? Map the emotional energy across the script. The best-performing scripts often follow: neutral hook → emotional recognition → logical proof → emotional close.

---

## Step 4: Repurposing Output

After decoding, generate new scripts using the same proven mechanisms. Structure every new script as:

```
HOOK: [exact opening line]
— why this hook works: [one sentence]

V1: [first value beat]
— objection removed: [name it]

V2: [second value beat]  
— psychological lever: [name it]

CTA: [close]
— friction level: [low/medium — explain]
```

Always generate:
- **2–3 proven-formula variants** (direct application of formulas found in the input scripts)
- **1–2 new-angle variants** (untested formulas not yet in the input set — label these clearly)

For new-angle variants, explain what makes them worth testing and what risk they carry.

---

## Step 5: Cross-Script Pattern Report (when 3+ scripts are provided)

When the user provides multiple scripts, add a patterns section:

- **Dominant hook formula**: Which formula appears most? What does that tell us about what works for this audience?
- **Consistent psychological triggers**: Which triggers appear across ALL scripts? These are your non-negotiables.
- **Missing triggers**: What proven triggers are absent from the set? These are your highest-upside test opportunities.
- **Structural consensus**: Is there a sentence-count, tempo, or sequence pattern that appears in all top performers?
- **Weakest link per script**: For each script, identify the single line most likely to lose the viewer. This is where repurposing should improve first.

---

## Output Format

Always structure your response in this order:

1. **HOOK DECODE** — the full Step 1 analysis, labeled clearly
2. **BODY DECODE** — Step 2 value chain
3. **FLOW & TEMPO** — Step 3 rhythm analysis
4. **PATTERN REPORT** — Step 5 (only if 3+ scripts)
5. **REPURPOSING OPTIONS** — Step 4 scripts, clearly formatted

If the user only provides a hook or one-liner (no full script), skip Steps 2 and 3, go deep on Step 1, and still generate repurposing variants in Step 4.

---

## Tone & Standards

- Be specific. "This creates curiosity" is not analysis. "This creates a curiosity gap because the viewer cannot determine whether this is an ad or a real story, forcing them to watch past second 3 to resolve the ambiguity" is analysis.
- Go psychological. Surface-level observations ("this is short and punchy") are useless. Name the mechanism in the brain.
- Be honest about what's weak. Top-spending scripts often have weak V2s or lazy CTAs. Say so.
- Always label which parts are proven (drawn from input scripts) vs. new angles (untested). The user needs to know what's a bet and what's a known quantity.
- Never confuse organic content analysis with paid ad analysis. These scripts are engineered to convert in a paid feed. Every word is intentional and accountable to a metric.
