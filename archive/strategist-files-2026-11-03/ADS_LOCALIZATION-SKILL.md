# ADS_LOCALIZATION.md

Guidelines for translating Bumpy ad scripts across languages and platforms.

**This is a separate document from LOCALIZATION.md** which covers in-app onboarding video only. Different job, different rules.

---

## Context: ads, not onboarding

The viewer has NOT downloaded the app. They're mid-scroll. You have 2 seconds to stop them.

The job is not to explain. It's to make them feel seen, then curious, then compelled.

**Ads tone:** platform-native, raw, like a real person talking — not a brand. Think creator content, not commercial.

---

## How ads differ from onboarding

| | Onboarding | Ads |
|---|---|---|
| User state | Just downloaded, curious | Scrolling, needs to be stopped |
| Job | Orient and guide | Hook, then convert |
| Tone | Warm, clear, welcoming | Punchy, native, scroll-stopping |
| Language | Clean casual | Platform-native, slang-heavy |
| Structure | Narrative arc across all lines | Hook-first, everything serves the hook |
| Voice | Friendly guide | Relatable person, peer-to-peer |

---

## Process for every language

1. **Identify the platform first.** TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts each have slightly different energy. TikTok is the rawest — creator-voice, direct address, slang-heavy. Reels skews slightly more polished. When in doubt, default to TikTok register.

2. **Find the hook.** The first line is everything. It needs to make the target audience feel directly seen. "Buat kamu yang single dan plus size" works because it calls out the viewer by identity. Every language version must nail this.

3. **Write peer-to-peer, not brand-to-consumer.** The narrator is a friend giving advice, not an app selling itself. First person ("gue", "yo", "ich") is expected. Avoid anything that sounds like a press release or app store copy.

4. **Use platform-native slang.** What words do people actually use on TikTok in this language? Use those. Not textbook vocabulary. Not formal equivalents.

5. **Rhythm over syllable perfection.** Syllable budget still applies (+2-4 per line max) but for ads, *spoken rhythm and energy* matter more than hitting an exact count. A line that punches harder at +5 syllables beats a flat line at exact count. Flag it, but don't sacrifice energy for budget.

6. **Staccato lines stay staccato.** Lines like "Hispanic men. Caribbean men." are written for punch — short, hammered, rhythmic. Don't merge them. Don't soften them. Find the equivalent energy in the target language.

7. **Keep English loanwords when locals use them.** Don't over-translate. If the audience says "curvy", "dating", "curves", "single" — keep them. Translating these makes the copy feel older and more foreign, not more local.

8. **Apply the user vs. supervisor lens.** Same as onboarding — evaluate flagged lines as a native user AND as a senior local copywriter. Surface both.

9. **Apply the Humanizer pass.** Check for translated-ese, soulless phrasing, AI vocabulary equivalents in the target language. The copy should sound like a specific person talking, not assembled text.

10. **Read it out loud at TikTok pace.** Fast. If it stumbles, rewrite.

11. **Localize the endscreen CTA too**, using the CTA confirmed for that persona. Give the English back-translation under it.

12. **Flag languages you're less sure of** (e.g. Turkish) for a native check before shipping.

## Language notes

### Indonesian (TikTok)
- Platform register: TikTok Gen Z Jakarta — mix of Bahasa + English freely, conversational fillers ("tuh", "loh", "kan"), direct address.
- "Cewek" not "wanita" — "wanita" is textbook, "cewek" is how real people talk.
- "Cowok" not "pria" — same reason.
- "Gue/lo" not "saya/kamu" — Jakarta Gen Z register. Use "kamu" only if the tone is slightly warmer/less street.
- "ini bisa buat" hits more direct than "gue mau bikin" for benefit statements.
- "digandrungi" is the right word for "highly desired/sought after" in this context — specific, confident, Gen Z.
- "GILA sama" = crazy about (intense attraction). "SUKA banget" = really likes. Both work; "gila" is more intense.
- Keep "curvy", "curves", "dating", "single", "plus size" in English — Indonesians say these words.
- Staccato nationality lines ("Cowok Hispanic. Cowok Caribbean.") — keep the punch, don't merge.
- Line 6 (nationality list) will run long (~17-18 syllables) — decide between keeping both nationalities for specificity or compressing to "cowok luar negeri" for rhythm. Client call.
- "Kalo" (not "kalau") and "deh" give the casual, talking-to-a-friend tone. "orang luar negeri" is how people casually say foreigners.

### Spanish LatAm (TikTok)
- Platform register: bestie-to-bestie advice energy. Girl talking to girl. Direct, warm, no corporate feel.
- "Chica" not "mujer" — "mujer" is too formal for TikTok register.
- "Hombres" works for staccato standalone lines — drop "los" for maximum punch ("Hombres hispanos." not "Los hombres hispanos.").
- "Caribeños." alone as one word — tightest, hardest punch for that line.
- "las más buscadas" over "lo más deseado" — active and confident, how a girl on TikTok would say it.
- Keep "curves", "curvy", "dating", "plus size", "single" in English — LatAm TikTok users say these.
- "AMAN las curves." — keeping "curves" in English is correct for this audience.
- Hook line: drop filler — "Si eres soltera y plus size" not "Si eres una chica soltera y plus size."
- Directive lines: lean and command-only — "Busca más lejos." with no warmup ("primero") hits harder as a standalone.
- "para de" vs "deja de" — both work, "para de" is slightly more casual/street.
- Gender-neutral callout to a mixed audience: masculine plural ("Mis cristianos solteros").
- Does not apply to Spain Spanish.

### Portuguese BR (TikTok)
- "do mundo todo" over "de todo o mundo" — the phrase real people use.
- Gender-neutral callout: masculine plural ("Meus cristãos solteiros").
- "Not local" phrasing performs: "Talvez Ele Não Seja Local", "E se o seu match for gringo? 😏".

### German (TikTok)
- Platform register: direct, cheeky, "unter uns" (between us) girl-to-girl energy. Never corporate.
- "Du" form always. No "Sie" ever in this context.
- Keep English loanwords Germans actually use: "Dating", "Curvy Girls", "Curves", "plus size", "single".
- "Curvy Girls" is what German TikTok says — not "Frauen mit Kurven" which sounds textbook.
- "das Maß aller Dinge" is the natural German idiom for "the gold standard" — use it, it lands clever to a German ear.
- "Schluss mit X!" is how Germans say "enough of this / stop doing X" — perfect for command lines, more punchy than "Hör auf zu...".
- Staccato nationality lines: use the shortest natural noun. "Latinos." alone over "Hispanische Männer." — adjective forms feel stiff as standalone punches.
- "Karibik-Männer." as a compound noun works well for the Caribbean line — very German construction, punchy standalone.
- "Die LIEBEN Curves." — "die" here is a pronoun (they), not an article. Natural spoken German for staccato follow-up line.
- German TikTok targeted at plus-size German women: "Latinos" is the right word for Hispanic men, same logic as "Gringos" in the Spanish LatAm version — avoid terms the target audience would identify with themselves.
- Line 5 ("das Maß aller Dinge") is idiomatic — fight for it over any literal translation.

### Japanese (TikTok)
- Platform register: casual feminine Japanese — beauty/lifestyle TikTok creator energy. Sharing a secret with the viewer. Short, warm, direct.
- **Use morae (拍) not syllables** — one mora ≈ one kana character. This is the correct rhythmic unit for Japanese VO timing.
- Never use です/ます form — that's TV commercial energy, wrong for TikTok.
- Sentence endings: よ, ね, じゃん, だから — these are natural casual feminine Japanese. Use them.
- "子" not "女性" when referring to women casually — "女性" is formal, "子" is how Japanese women talk about each other.
- "みんな" (everyone) over "彼ら" (they) — warmer, more natural in spoken Japanese.
- "狙う" (target/aim for) over "探す" (search) for dating context — more casual and punchy.
- Katakana for foreign concepts: "プラスサイズ", "カーブ", "マッチング", "ラテン系", "アフリカ系".
- Mixing English in katakana context is native to Japanese TikTok — "カーブLOVEだから。" is how young Japanese women actually write/speak.
- Staccato nationality lines: drop "の男性" entirely — "アフリカ系。" and "ラテン系。" alone is understood in context and hits the punch.
- "Stop dating locally" concept: frame as stopping pursuit of Japanese men specifically — this is culturally loaded (body image pressure) and lands as cultural escape, not just geography. "日本人だけ狙うのやめて。" captures this.
- "教えるね" at end of benefit lines — "I'll tell you" is very natural creator speech in Japanese, builds curiosity.
- Lines about the gold standard and stop dating locally will run long (~13-15 morae vs 7-9) — structurally unavoidable. Flag to audio editor.
- Target audience: plus-size Japanese women in Japan struggling with local dating standards.
- African men → "アフリカ系", Latino men → "ラテン系" — these are the natural Japanese terms.
- 運命の人は近くにいるとは限らない ("your destined person isn't necessarily nearby") was the best-paying Meta headline in the account (Oct 2026).

### Thai (TikTok)
- Question form with ไหม reads native ("อยากมีเพื่อนต่างชาติไหม?"); "เลย" adds the casual push. Keep "Bumpy" in Latin script.

### Turkish
- Default endscreen: `Uluslararası ruh eşini bul. Bumpy.` "dünyanın dört bir yanından" (from all over the world) reads warmer than a literal "uluslararası". Always get a native check.
