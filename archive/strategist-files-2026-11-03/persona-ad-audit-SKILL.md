# Persona Ad Audit

A method for figuring out, with real rigor, what a persona actually wants — then checking
that against what your ads have actually said to them — so the gap between the two
becomes a list of concrete, ready-to-brief creative options.

The method has four stages, run in this order, but held **holistically**: research must
visibly show up in the gap analysis and in the final copy, or it was decorative. A skill
run that produces beautiful research and then generic marketing-speak options has failed,
even if each stage looks good in isolation.

```
STAGE 1: Ad Audit  →  STAGE 2: Deep Persona Research  →  STAGE 3: Gap Analysis  →  STAGE 4: Angle/Copy Options
```

---

## Stage 1: Ad Audit

**Input:** the user's existing ad creative for this persona — ideally ranked by spend
(highest to lowest), since spend is the strongest signal of what the algorithm has
already validated as working for this audience.

**Special case — short-form/social ads with low average watch time:** if the user notes
that average watch time is very low (e.g. ~2 seconds), treat the hook/first-frame as the
entire ad. Do not force a body/CTA breakdown on creative that never gets watched that far.
Say so explicitly rather than inventing structure that isn't there.

For each ad, extract exactly two things — no more, no less, at this stage:

| Ad (ranked by spend) | Awareness Level | Angle |
|---|---|---|

**Awareness Level** — use Schwartz's five levels, but translate them concretely to the
platform reality:
1. **Unaware** — no problem or category named. Often pure fantasy/aspiration visuals.
2. **Problem Aware** — names or implies the pain, doesn't mention a category solution.
3. **Solution Aware** — implies or names that a category of solution exists, not the brand.
4. **Product Aware** — the brand/product is named or shown; viewer hasn't decided to act.
5. **Most Aware** — brand named, clear value claim, clear CTA. Ready to convert, just needs the push.

**Angle** — treat angle, motivation, and offer as one fused thing, not three. The angle
*is* the psychological mechanism (name it precisely: "grievance validation," "peer-status
flex," "movement legitimacy" — not vague labels like "emotional") **and** whatever it's
landing on, whether that's pure belief-reinforcement with no ask, or a concrete value
claim/CTA. Note the landing point as part of the angle description itself — e.g. "grievance
validation, no ask" vs. "rational value comparison → direct CTA" — rather than splitting it
into a separate column. Most ad sets will have very few angles that land on a concrete
claim/CTA at all — note this explicitly when it's true, it's usually itself a gap (see Stage 3).

Do not spend excessive effort re-decoding hook mechanics ad-by-ad here (that's a different
skill — see ad-script-decoder if it's available). This audit stays at the strategic layer:
awareness/angle/offer, ranked, so it can be compared against research next.

## Stage 2: Deep Persona Research

This is the stage most likely to get skipped or done shallowly. Don't. A gap analysis is
only as good as the research it's checked against.

**This stage has a required output shape — do not substitute a looser summary for it.**
Prose guidance about "being holistic" tends to drift into disconnected bullet lists across
different runs; the fixed scaffold below is what actually keeps it connected. Write it as:

1. **Segment split** — name if the persona is actually 2+ distinct people hiding under one
   label (it usually is). Give each a short handle and one line on what makes their entry
   psychology different, even if they land in the same funnel.
2. **Psychology, zoomed in, as one paragraph** — start from base human psychology, narrow
   to gender-specific psychology if relevant, narrow again to this specific persona. Don't
   jump straight to persona-specific claims with no foundation under them.
3. **The "true why," numbered and brutal** — 4-6 numbered statements of the persona's real
   motivation, stripped of the noble/socially-acceptable version. If a drafted line still
   sounds flattering, push it one layer more honest before finalizing it.
4. **One closing sentence naming "the engine"** — the single underlying driver everything
   else has to connect back to. Every angle generated in Stage 4 should be checkable
   against this one sentence.

Beyond that required shape, also research and synthesize the following as part of the same
connected picture (not five separate lists):

1. **Base human psychology** — what does any human, regardless of persona, respond to
   (status, loss aversion, identity confirmation, social proof, curiosity gaps)?
2. **Gender-specific psychology, if relevant** — how does this general psychology express
   differently for this persona's gender? (e.g., for men, romantic outcomes often function
   as a public status signal among peers, not just a private preference.)
3. **Persona-specific "true why," stated bluntly** — what is this persona's actual
   motivation, even if it isn't the noble or socially acceptable version of the story? If
   research or self-analysis produces a flattering answer, push past it and ask what the
   less flattering, more honest layer underneath is. This is the single highest-leverage
   step — generic personas produce generic ads.
4. **Desires, dreams, pains** — what does this persona want to become, what are they
   afraid of, what's the unspoken frustration in their head day to day?
5. **Why the winning ads actually won** — connect points 1-4 to Stage 1's audit. If an ad
   got heavy spend, the algorithm found a real nerve — don't just note that it worked,
   explain *which* psychological driver it hit and why.

**Critically: go find where this persona actually talks, in their own words.**
Web-search for the persona's community — forums, subreddits, comment sections, niche
publications written *about* them or *by* them. Extract and hold onto:

- **Their actual self-identity** — how do they describe themselves, and just as
  importantly, what labels do they reject or resent being lumped into? (Misreading this
  is a fast way to produce copy that repels instead of resonates.)
- **Their internal vocabulary** — specific terms, slang, or shorthand the community
  actually uses. This needs to survive into Stage 4 literally, not get translated into
  generic marketing language. Research that never touches the final copy was wasted.
- **Who they actually are demographically/psychographically**, especially where it
  contradicts the stereotype your existing ads may be assuming (e.g. richer/poorer, more
  or less diverse, older/younger than the visual archetype currently in rotation).
- **Segment splits** — most personas are not one person. Look for at least two distinct
  sub-motivations or life stages within the "single" persona (e.g. someone optimizing from
  a position of strength vs. someone escaping from a position of exhaustion/pain). Note
  which sub-segment your current ads visually and tonally target, and which is underserved.

## Stage 3: Gap Analysis

Cross-reference Stage 1 against Stage 2 directly. This is not a new brainstorm — it's a
literal diff between what's been said and what the research says could be said.

Produce:

1. **Awareness coverage map** — which of the 5 levels are covered, which are empty or thin?
2. **Angle coverage map** — list every angle actually used (from Stage 1), then list every
   angle Stage 2's research surfaces as available but unused. Be exhaustive here — the
   angle list should be long, not a token 2-3 items. Since angle now includes its landing
   point (belief-reinforcement vs. concrete claim/CTA), explicitly call out the ratio —
   e.g. "5 belief/fantasy angles landing on nothing, 1 angle landing on a concrete claim."
   A lopsided ratio is almost always a real gap worth flagging on its own.
3. **Segment coverage** — which sub-segment(s) from Stage 2 are visually/tonally served,
   which are missing entirely?
4. **Vocabulary/trust coverage** — is the community's actual self-identity and language
   present anywhere in current creative, or is the brand speaking *about* them in outsider
   language? Note this as its own gap line — it's usually a free, high-leverage fix.

State the single biggest gap in one plain sentence before listing everything else. This
is the sentence a creative lead should be able to repeat back from memory.

---

## Stage 4: Angle & Copy Options

Generate as many angle options as the gap analysis supports — err toward more, not fewer.
Do not box each option into a single rigid awareness-level/angle pairing if a mixed
approach is more useful; note when an option deliberately blends levels or angles.

For **every** option, give:

- **Gap it fills** — one line, referencing Stage 3 directly.
- **Awareness level** it's aimed at.
- **Angle** — stated as one fused thing (mechanism + landing point), e.g. "SMV-mismatch
  grievance → risk-reversal offer," not split into separate angle/motivation/offer fields.
- **In-ad copy example** — an actual line of copy, not a description of copy. This is the
  part a creative team can act on directly.

**Non-negotiable copy standards:**

- **Use the community's real vocabulary from Stage 2, literally, where it fits.** If
  research surfaced specific insider terms, at least some options must use them. Sparingly
  enough not to alienate the broader/quieter majority of the audience, but present.
- **Never write copy so generic it could apply to any product in the category.** If a
  persona would react "duh, it's just [generic product]" instead of "hell yeah, this is
  for me" — rewrite it. Every option should be unmistakably about this specific persona
  and this specific product, not dating-app-in-general, not fitness-app-in-general, etc.
- **Name the persona's specific situation, identity, or self-label directly in copy
  where possible**, rather than writing something that could quietly also target a
  generic version of the customer.
- Organize final output by awareness level so a creative team can see coverage at a
  glance, but keep the list flexible/mixable rather than rigidly boxed — call out
  explicitly when an option deliberately spans two levels or blends two angles.

---

## Output Format

Always deliver, in this order:

1. **Ad Audit table** (Stage 1)
2. **Persona Research summary** (Stage 2) — written as one connected narrative, not four
   disconnected headers; explicitly include the community vocabulary and self-identity
   findings, not just abstract psychology
3. **Gap Analysis** (Stage 3) — coverage maps + the one-sentence biggest gap
4. **Angle & Copy Options** (Stage 4) — as many as the research supports, grouped by
   awareness level, each with gap-filled / awareness level / in-ad copy

## Tone & Standards

- Be brutally honest about the persona's real motivations, even the unflattering layer.
  Softening this into the noble version produces weak, generic creative.
- Research is not decoration. If a finding from Stage 2 doesn't show up anywhere in Stage
  3 or Stage 4, either cut it or go back and use it — don't let stages run in isolation.
- Specificity beats breadth in copy. One sharp line naming the persona's real situation
  beats three vague lines that could apply to anyone.
- When in doubt about whether copy is generic, apply the test: would this persona feel
  "called out" (in a good way) reading this, or could they scroll past assuming it's not
  for them? If the latter, it needs another pass.
